dear one,

i’m collaging some letters for you.  i’ve been quiet against my own volition, writing to you so many times, yet my hand always stopping me. life, chaos, doubt, despair, myriad distractions… there is always something and it is often so small and self-consuming.  

but i’m on my couch now, lying in soft greyed light, listening to the radiator rattle. i take another breath to re-enter my body again. the first breath back must always be very deep.  it carves a space so that knots can loosen. it carves a space so that whatever tension is invariably stored in my chest must open up, making way for air to fully circle in and out of my lungs.

or maybe i’m in my bedroom, the home where i grew up in. and i’m listening to the patter of freezing rain, contemplating the changes of phase that somehow always surprise me this time of year.  i exhale and think of last week when the weather had such brilliant temperateness.  i roamed outside much longer than anticipated; i felt my spirit thaw in the sunlight.  and still there are parts of me hesitant to melt in turn, to trust whatever new is coming. ice clings to the edges of those inner places, like the old dirty snowbanks of my street, keeping hope or expectation at a distance. which is okay, i’m realizing. i’m moving slowly back into the world, granting space for small indulgence, cradling both passion and pain in my palms.

and my shoulders lower, just a bit, to loosen where they became tight. or i widen my feet, such that my toes fully grasp the floor, and feel the force of the earth pushing back onto me. my arch, tender, taut, vulnerable, becomes a portal again.  to my physicality, my frailness, my carbon-based nature. 

one of my close neighbors is a large gingko tree outside of our building, the tips of which i can see when looking out my bedroom window.  the gusts of wind have been making her barren branches shudder to and fro.  watching them for a while, seeing her wave so freely, struck me as shockingly beautiful.  maybe because the more you look at something, there are only two ways it can go; it either appears stranger, more distant, bizarre, eerie and distasteful — or, with each view, more and more beautiful. as in: before you realize you have already begun to revere it.

i look again, and before i know it, its branches are bursting with green. we are beyond the midpoint of april. beyond two months of missiles and bombing, or two years of genocidal rampage, or four years of nightmarish proxy war. i have been crumbling over and over again, under the weight of the world. i taste chalk in my mouth, struggling to speak, hurting those i love most with my silence. <em>[must every place-name on earth / be a shorthand for violence / on a map of grief?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pbg8Q0nYPxU)</em> and yet, the flush of blooming trees take over the streets around me. i still don’t know how to believe in spring, living on ground so soaked in blood. <em>but there is no poem unless i / we can find the courage to speak</em>.

only two weeks ago the trees were still bare but for the tiniest of buds, hinting to changes inevitable. i still don’t know how to believe in change that is inevitable. there is the sticky languidity of summer’s heat and bone-chilling interminable winter. there are twisted scripts of violence and rhetoric saturating so much of what we hear, clouding imagination, exhausting resolve, distracting from the truth — truth that is love and beauty and strength and kindness ever flowing all around us, through us and our relationships and our communities, if we allow ourselves to be witness and agent and vessel for it. so i am trying to remind myself to think about how we might name tomorrow. how tomorrow might be a place we can name after something that grows.

tomorrow, my piano teacher turns 91 years old. i held his hand yesterday, shocked by the feathery texture of his skin.  it was skin of a body with only so much time left, which we all knew. 

he lay in his bed — tired of still being in this world and weathering loss after loss. and he smiled with a glint in his eye, listening eagerly to updates and stories and music. i saw his massive collection of film noirs and documentaries, that another former student had graciously and thoroughly compiled to be more accessible for him on the tv. 

certainly, a poem lives there. it was so easy to hear, whether in his crooked grin or intensity of focus, the slow flutter of his eyelids, the heaviness of sighs. he steps into tomorrow as he steps into every day, with the humble attitude of a true student: that simple desire to continue learning, listening, growing into the new. tomorrow i will name after him.
