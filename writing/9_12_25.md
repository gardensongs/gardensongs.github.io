dear friend,

i feel it’s been a long time since i’ve written a letter to you, specifically — this struck me as i was walking back home from walgreens the other day, bracing myself against thick rain droplets from an ominously shrouded sky.  there is <a href="https://chipmnk.substack.com/" target="_blank">a newsletter</a> that i’ve subscribed to for years now (back when tinyletter was still alive; the writer has since begrudgingly moved to substack), and my favorite thing about it is the consistent personalia. personalty? i’m trying to find the word that simply means, <em>to be personal</em>. in that when he writes, there is never a particular thesis or artful intention in mind. there is just his life, and what is going on; there are his frustrations and his small joys, his work, his body, his friends, his crushes, his crushing losses. each letter he writes is the fulfilment of a promise. they arrive every week, and the promise is only to be present, however that may look — the promise is to bear witness to life, and to end with hope: first and foremost for the wellness of others and, if manageable, for himself as well. which sometimes he does not manage to do. but he will always, always end his letters with that benediction, “i hope you’re okay.”

on the corner of st. johns and albany, i thought of you. i looked up and found a pair of buildings, slabs of stone that the still setting sun was able to touch.  they were sharp and soft in the red. the light felt like a miracle, as it so often does to me. i thought of you, how much i loved you, how much i hoped that you were okay, and how i needed to write you soon. 

then a week passed (as it somehow always does…haha). a week and some distance being enough to bring a new chill to the evening air. have you also felt a change of seasons? i returned to brooklyn from wisconsin three mondays ago, and it was as if i entered a whole new world. a bit silly, i know, because i was only gone for two and a half weeks. such is a length of time that theoretically seems long; but then you live it, and each day disappears more quickly than the last.  and yet, it’s enough that memory of the quotidian becomes fuzzy. life has room to surprise you — you hang on the precipice of a new reality, and if your timing is right, maybe some invisible threshold is finally crossed. like: the weather noticeably shifting. a thought being released. a habit lodging itself. the collapse of choices that make up decision.

i feel change somewhere in my bones, but superficially still struggle to believe it or keep up as i think i should. in the way that snow can still exist on a sixty degree day in february: it is a slow and sticky transition of phase.  i wish i could be magnanimous and patient about it, but i’m really not. more than anything, god, i’m just tired! and i run around trying to find new ways of coping with the ever humbling truth that sometimes, shifts unfold slowly and you cannot control their pace. 

sweet yuni bought me <a href="https://bookshop.org/p/books/katabasis-deluxe-limited-edition-a-novel-r-f-kuang/38f8659a854e4fb4?ean=9780063021471&amp;next=t&amp;" target="_blank">a book</a> in which the magick of the world derives its power from paradoxes: or rather, from whole-hearted belief in the illusions that paradoxes momentarily make real. for example, consider a mound of sand, with its number of grains appearing infinite. you take a single grain, and what of it — the change in the mound is minuscule, practically nothing. one grain is effectively meaningless in the context of the millions of grains left behind. but where lies the contour of meaning: how many grains can you take until the mound is significantly changed from one state to the one immediately following? in a way, isn’t it true that the mound of sand cannot change at all? 

well… of course not, because a grain is a grain and a billion is a billion; when you take 500 million grains from a billion-grain mound, no matter how drawn out or tedious that process is, you will be left with half of what you started. it’s a grounding in logic that encapsulates how i’ve been trying to remind myself of what’s *really* true. when i despair that my efforts at living or changing or growing or showing up with compassion in this endlessly cruel world are minuscule in relation to my griefs — not falling for the paradox. the material is irrefutable. shifts, however small, are shifts that invariably lead to something new. whether that's a shift into kindness, patience, acceptance, or courage — wherever one is called to move into.

what i hope most for you is: that whatever shifts are making their way through your life, they are making a way for goodness. i hope they begin to feel like a flower suddenly blooming. you deserve so much good! that's all i really wanted to say ♥︎

love,<br>
e
